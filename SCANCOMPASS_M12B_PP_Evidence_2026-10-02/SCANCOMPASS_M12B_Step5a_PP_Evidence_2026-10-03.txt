SECOND-REVIEW FINDING A — MID-READ ABORT AND CLEANUP
Previously the bounded reader checked the signal only before reading. A request
aborted during a pending read could remain pending and later return success.

The corrected reader:
- subscribes to request.signal;
- calls reader.cancel() on abort so the pending read settles;
- re-checks the signal after listener attachment;
- checks the abort state before another read and before accepting EOF;
- returns UNREADABLE rather than accepting cancellation-generated EOF;
- requests cancellation without awaiting indefinitely pending producer cleanup;
- observes cancellation rejection without leaving an unhandled rejection;
- guards getReader(), returning UNREADABLE for an already-locked body;
- does not cancel or release somebody else's reader;
- removes its listener and releases the reader it acquired;
- retains actual-byte counting and the inclusive 2,048-byte accepted-body ceiling.

The bound applies to retained/accepted input, not to the physical size of a chunk
already supplied by the transport. Detecting overflow can require receiving the
chunk that crosses the ceiling.

SECOND-REVIEW FINDING B — ACTUAL ACCOUNT-ASSOCIATION WINDOW
The earlier test deleted the account before calling reset completion. It did not
exercise the preliminary-read-to-account-lock window.

The new required regression:
1. Creates accounts A and B and a challenge initially belonging to A.
2. Holds A's account-row lock on a blocker connection.
3. Starts completion and observes a lock-waiting backend.
4. Reassigns the unlocked challenge row to B while completion is blocked.
5. Releases the blocker and requires CHALLENGE_INVALID.
6. Asserts unchanged credentials, sessions and checked lockout state for A/B,
   and that the moved challenge is neither consumed nor deleted.

The production association guard was already present and remains unchanged.
A new structural assertion specifically requires challenge.accountId !== account.id
before mutation. Additional structural assertions require writes to use the locked
account.id rather than trusting an unlocked preliminary value.

This review inspected the committed PostgreSQL regression but did not independently
execute it against PostgreSQL. Its successful execution and mutation detection are
recorded by the implementation in the milestone document.

THE CHAIN

  1. DISCOVERED
     PR #19's ten repeated acceptance runs exposed a real defect in run 7:
     concurrent wrong passwords lost a durable failure increment, observed as
     "expected 6, actual 5". PR #19 deliberately did NOT fix it.
     -> SCANCOMPASS_BASELINE_RELIABILITY_PP_Evidence_2026-10-05.txt
     -> SCANCOMPASS_LOGIN_LOCKOUT_RACE_FINDING_2026-10-05.txt

  2. CORRECTED IN PR #20
     The login route captured the time before the slow hashing step; the row
     lock serialised the writes but the timestamps arrived out of order, and a
     negative elapsed time made the policy restart the observation window. The
     clock is now read UNDER the row lock, by the store that owns the write.
     Reproduced first on the old code: 14 failures in 20 for the original test,
     and 5 of 5 for a forced interleaving. Thirteen new concurrency tests; the
     lockout sections passed 20 of 20.
     -> SCANCOMPASS_LOGIN_LOCKOUT_FIX_PP_Evidence_2026-10-05.txt

  3. RETENTION FAILURES INVESTIGATED
     PR #20's own twenty acceptance runs were 17 PASS and 3 FAIL, and ALL THREE
     FAILURES WERE RETENTION, NOT THE LOCKOUT CORRECTION. The cause was unknown
     at the time and was recorded as open, not waived as a flake.
     -> SCANCOMPASS_LOGIN_LOCKOUT_FIX_PP_Evidence_2026-10-05.txt section 7

  4. A REAL RETENTION DEFECT FOUND
     Sixty full acceptance runs alternating between PR #19 and PR #20 gave the
     same failure rate on both, so PR #20 had not introduced it. With the
     received value captured, a batch limited to 2 was found to have deleted 3
     rows, and another run deleted all 5. The timeout hypothesis was disproved
     by measurement. Root cause: a LIMIT inside a sub-select bounds each
     execution of that sub-select, not the DELETE around it, and a
     nested-loop plan re-executes it. ONLY QUALIFYING ROWS WERE EVER DELETED —
     the defect was the work bound, not the deletion scope. The diagnostic
     committed no code and made no product fix.
     -> SCANCOMPASS_RETENTION_FLAKE_DIAGNOSIS_2026-10-05.txt

  5. CORRECTED IN PR #21
     Each batch became one transaction: a top-level candidate SELECT whose
     LIMIT is final, the ids materialised in application memory, then a DELETE
     naming exactly those ids and repeating the predicate, with fail-closed
     invariant checks that roll the batch back rather than report a short
     count. The expiry sweep keeps its non-blocking lock; the purge keeps its
     blocking one. No schema, migration or grant change.
     -> SCANCOMPASS_RETENTION_BATCH_BOUND_FIX_PP_Evidence_2026-10-05.txt

  6. 30 / 30 COMBINED ACCEPTANCE
     Thirty consecutive acceptance runs on the committed runner at PR #21's fix
     commit: 30 PASS, 0 FAIL. Readiness 30 of 30; retention 45 of 45 in every
     run, including the test that had failed 3 of 20; the lockout sections 19
     of 19 in every run; the restore drill 30 of 30; cleanup verified 30 of 30.
     The record states plainly that the thirty runs are NOT the primary proof —
     the deterministic forced-plan regression is.

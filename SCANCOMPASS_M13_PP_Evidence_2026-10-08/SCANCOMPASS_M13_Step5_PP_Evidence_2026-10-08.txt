3. WHAT THE INTERFACE IS

A product summary and a purchase button inside the signed-in account area, plus
the two pages the provider sends a browser back to. The purchase button sends
exactly one field — the domain — to one same-origin path, and hands the
returned payment address straight to a browser navigation. [S7]

What the browser never receives, asserted by automated boundary tests rather
than by inspection: no price, no currency, no price identifier, no API key, no
signing secret, no provider mode, no product code, no account or order
identifier, no payment-provider software-development kit, no publishable key and
no public payment variable of any kind. The payment address is used once and
stored nowhere — not in page state, browser storage, an attribute, the page
address or a log. [S7]

5. THE API-VERSION PROBLEM, WHICH NEARLY STOPPED THE STEP (SOURCE-RECORDED) [S7]

The application requires inbound provider messages to be formatted as one
pinned API version and refuses anything else. The first real messages arrived as
an OLDER version, and the application did exactly what it was built to do:
refused them, wrote nothing, and did not consume the message identifier, so they
remained redeliverable.

Four configurations of the provider's local forwarding tool were tested before
the cause was found: the tool resolves "latest version" lazily, and its startup
banner is the only reliable indication of what it will actually send. Once it
reported the expected version, the real message was accepted and recorded at the
pinned version.

THE PIN WAS NEVER WEAKENED TO MAKE THE TEST PASS. Two findings are carried
forward: the provider's local forwarding tool does NOT honour a version pinned
on a provider-side endpoint, so the design's assumption about where that version
is configured does not hold for local testing — though it does for the live
endpoint, which carries its own version set at creation; and a lapsed sign-in
for that tool stops forwarding SILENTLY, which is exactly the condition the
design calls alarm-worthy. Both were recovered by replaying the messages, which
is the redeliverability the design relies on. [S7]

6. WHAT WAS NOT EXERCISED, AND WHY (SOURCE-RECORDED, OWNER DECISION) [S7,R1]

Carried honestly rather than quietly closed. In each case what stands in its
place is named.

  A PROVIDER-FIRST DEFERRED REVERSAL was NOT practically exercised. The exact
  test schedule was explained to the owner in advance, and the owner DECLINED
  it on the grounds that provider delivery order is nondeterministic, so the
  schedule might not reproduce. What stands in its place is M13-4's
  eleven-case ordered-delivery matrix, driven by locally signed messages
  through the real transport and transaction against real PostgreSQL. [R1]

  THE THREE-HOUR STALE-CHECKOUT SWEEP TIMING was not exercised in real time,
  because it would require waiting three hours. The provider's own
  session-expiry path WAS exercised and reached the same state by a different
  mechanism; the timing rule itself is proven by M13-4 against real PostgreSQL.

  NO DISPUTE was exercised: a chargeback cannot be raised on demand in test
  mode without provider tooling this acceptance did not have. The dispute paths
  are covered by the same M13-4 matrix.

  NO COMPLETED ANONYMOUS FREE SCAN was exercised in the disposable environment.
  Two reasons, neither payment-related: the launch gate has four unrelated
  reverse-proxy and hosting blockers in that environment, and the scanner
  worker's address is a compiled-in container service name that does not
  resolve on a developer machine. What stands in its place is the
  byte-identical scanning response across every payment state, plus the
  automated proof that the scanning and report paths reach no payment module.

  THE TWO PAYMENT READINESS STATUSES EXIST BUT NOTHING SURFACES THEM. No boot
  log line and no health field reports them, so the design's requirement that
  the "stop selling, keep reconciling" posture be legible at a glance, and the
  live blocker requiring the two reconciliation queues to be WATCHED, have no
  mechanism yet. Proving the two statuses for this acceptance required
  evaluating the functions directly. Recorded as an M13-6 item.

  AN OBSERVATION MADE AND DELIBERATELY NOT ACTED ON: a real refund message was
  inspected and DOES carry the order identifier the checkout attaches. The
  design treats that as a possible future shortcut, not as authority, and NO
  CODE WAS CHANGED to start relying on it. One confirmed observation is not
  grounds for making a provider field an authoritative key.

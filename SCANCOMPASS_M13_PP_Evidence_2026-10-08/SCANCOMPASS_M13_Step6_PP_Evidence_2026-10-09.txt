5. F-1 — THE ONE DEFECT, AND THE OWNER'S DECISION TO FIX IT

THE DEFECT. A signature-valid provider message whose own mode differed from the
mode the server had been told to expect was RECORDED AND ACKNOWLEDGED rather
than refused. Recording it consumed the message's provider identifier, so the
corrected redelivery of that same message was then treated as a duplicate and
did nothing. The end state was money taken, nothing granted, and the case in
NEITHER reconciliation queue — the one outcome the whole milestone is built to
make impossible. It can only arise from a configuration fault, which is exactly
the family of fault the design elsewhere refuses to record for this reason.
[S3]

THE FIRST DISPOSITION, AND WHY IT WAS WITHDRAWN. The review's first pass did
NOT change the behaviour: a webhook behaviour change was outside M13-6's scope,
and the standing instruction for a defect of that kind is to stop and report
rather than redesign. It was instead guarded in the runbook, in the
configuration template and in the design's live-mode blocker list. THE OWNER
REJECTED THAT DISPOSITION, on the ground that Decision A assigns the long-lived
rollback floor and MUST NOT BLESS A KNOWN PAYMENT-LOSS BEHAVIOUR AS THE FLOOR.
The first pass's wording is kept in the source record, labelled historical, and
is not deleted. [S3]

THE FIX, ON THE SAME PULL REQUEST. A message of the undeclared mode is now
refused BEFORE ANY TRANSACTION IS OPENED — no lock, no read, no row — and the
HTTP layer answers a retryable 503 with one closed log line. Because the check
lives in the function rather than at the HTTP edge, every caller is protected,
and the transaction re-asserts it as defence in depth. The separate check on a
message that contradicts ITS OWN ORDER'S recorded mode is unchanged and still
records a mismatch with a 2xx. No schema, migration, grant or outcome-set
change. [S3]

PROVEN AT FOUR LEVELS, and the proof includes the redelivery that the defect
broke: on a disposable PostgreSQL 18.6 database, as the application role,
through the real transport and transaction, a message delivered under the wrong
declaration was refused 503 WITH ZERO ROWS WRITTEN and the order untouched;
redelivered again under the same wrong declaration, refused again; then THE
SAME provider identifier, with a byte-identical body and an identical signature
header, delivered once the declaration matched, was FULFILLED — order paid, one
available entitlement — and only a further identical redelivery after that
answered the ordinary duplicate. The reverse direction was refused the same way.
[S3]

A MUTATION CHECK SHOWS THE TESTS BITE: with the three source files reverted to
the pre-fix code, both new integration cases fail with "expected 200 to be
503"; restored, they pass. [S3]

THE FLOOR NOW REQUIRES THE FIX. The rollback floor gained a fifth property —
"carries the F-1 correction" — with a mechanical check against the built image,
and a test pins every string the floor check greps for to the executable source
where its property lives. The earlier M13-4 merge therefore NO LONGER QUALIFIES
as a floor, and the first qualifying source is the merge of this corrected pull
request. NO COMMIT IDENTIFIER WAS INVENTED to express that: the floor is named
by property, and the concrete image could only be assigned at Decision A. [S3]

7. PROVIDER MODE IS IMMUTABLE — A WITHDRAWN PROCEDURE, RECORDED

The F-1 fix made a second problem visible, and the owner's SECOND re-review
found the first answer to it insufficient. [S3]

The first answer was a procedure for draining one provider mode before
switching to the other. The owner's objection: it drained CHECKOUT SESSIONS,
but a paid order is never swept, and ITS refunds and disputes keep arriving on
the old mode's destination for as long as either can be raised. A process
serving one declared mode and one secret would refuse those messages — now
retryably, thanks to F-1 — until the provider's retries simply ran out.

THE RESOLUTION IS A RULE, NOT A PROCEDURE: provider mode is IMMUTABLE for the
production payment database. Test-mode acceptance of the exact candidate image
runs OFF production, in an isolated environment with its own database; Decision
A declares no mode at all; and Decision B, if it is ever taken, opens LIVE
DIRECTLY and never declares test mode in production, before or after go-live,
not even as an incident switch — the incident switch is "stop selling, keep
reconciling". Concurrent test and live operation would need a separately
reviewed architecture. The withdrawn drain survives only as the narrower
request-byte drain it was always valid for. [S3]

THE WITHDRAWN TEXT IS KEPT IN THE SOURCE RECORD, LABELLED WITHDRAWN. Two status
sentences elsewhere in the repository that claimed "no blocker in merged code"
were also corrected to say what the review actually found: one blocker, F-1,
fixed on this pull request, and no other. [S3]

9. THE TWO DECISIONS, KEPT APART — THE CENTRAL OUTPUT OF THIS STEP

The runbook M13-6 added states two decisions and refuses to merge them.

  DECISION A — DEPLOY M13 WITH PAYMENTS CLOSED. Changes the application and
  maintenance images, applies migration 0004 (three new, EMPTY tables), applies
  the M13 grants, and writes explicit payments-off lines into the production
  configuration. USER-VISIBLE EFFECT: NOTHING. Both payment routes answer 404
  for every method and no purchase interface renders. The payment era stays
  ERA 1, because no order, event or entitlement can exist. Rollback is by
  image.

  DECISION B — OPEN PAYMENTS. Raises the payment flags, installs provider
  credentials, creates a LIVE event destination and installs the web-server
  webhook location. It moves the payment era to ERA 2 PERMANENTLY, from the
  moment anything is opened. It is written as a GATED CHECKLIST AND DELIBERATELY
  NOT AS A SCRIPT, because most of its inputs do not exist yet.

COMPLETING A DOES NOT AUTHORISE B. That sentence is in the runbook, in the
design record and in the evidence record, because it is the single most
misreadable fact in the milestone. [S2,S3,S4]

AT M13-6, DECISION A WAS PREPARED AND NOT EXECUTED, and Decision B was blocked
by every open live-mode item. Every financial or customer-affecting action in
the runbook — a refund, a revocation, opening checkout, going live — is marked
an owner decision, and nothing in the repository issues a refund. [S4]

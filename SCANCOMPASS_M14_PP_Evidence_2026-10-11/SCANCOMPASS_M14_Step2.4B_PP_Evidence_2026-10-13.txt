2. WHAT M14-2.4B IS, AND WHAT IT IS NOT
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS. The fifth of the ten paid checks, as a pure function, plus the
reference table it needs. The question it asks: DOES THE CERTIFICATE THE SITE
SERVES AGREE WITH THE RECORDS SAYING WHICH AUTHORITY MAY ISSUE FOR THAT NAME?
If it does not, renewal may one day fail — which is the finding's actual value
to an operator, and the reason the locked memo frames it as a renewal concern
rather than as a compromise. [S2]

IT NEVER REPORTS A FAILURE, BY DESIGN, EVEN BEFORE THE OWNER DECISION. The most
it could ever say was an informational warning. A disagreement between a
certificate and these records is an OPERATIONAL RISK, NOT A BREACH, and the
check was built that way from the start. [S2]

WHAT IT RECEIVES. Only facts already in the engine's hands: the existing walk of
the authorisation records, the final secure response's certificate evidence, and
step 2.3's two attribution facts — which host served the certificate and where
the record walk began. NO RESOLVER, CLIENT, ADDRESS, BODY OR CLOCK PARAMETER,
pinned by a boundary test. [S2]

THREE IDENTITIES ARE KEPT APART THROUGHOUT, AND CONFLATING ANY TWO WOULD BE A
DEFECT. The host that served the certificate; the host the record walk started
at, which is the authorised host; and the host that actually OWNS the record set
found, which may be an ancestor because the walk climbs. THE COMPARISON HAPPENS
ONLY WHEN THE FIRST TWO ARE EQUAL, AND THE THIRD IS NEVER THE COMPARISON KEY.
[S2]

WHAT IT IS NOT. Not wired into anything. Not the remaining five checks. Not any
of the five open owner semantic decisions, which it touches none of. Not a
change to the free certificate evidence, the transport, the record walk, the
network authority, the worker protocol, any score, any report or any version —
the owner's decision text forbids each of those explicitly, and this session
confirmed the diff contains none of them. [R1,S2,O1]

3. THE AUTHORITY TABLE, AND WHY IT IS ONE ROW LONG
================================================================================

THE PROBLEM IT EXISTS TO SOLVE. The transport records the issuing certificate's
common name — in practice a short intermediate name. The authorisation records
name an authority by a DOMAIN it publishes for that purpose. THE TWO ARE
DIFFERENT KINDS OF STRING, and a table is the only bridge. [S2]

HOW THE TABLE IS CONSTRAINED, WHICH MATTERS MORE THAN WHAT IS IN IT. Matching is
EXACT, byte for byte: no case folding, no trimming, no substring, no prefix, no
organisation name and no certificate-name fallback. The table is CLOSED, so
anything unlisted abstains. It is VERSION-STAMPED and DEEP-FROZEN at load. Each
row lists EVERY identifier its authority documents, and a row with more than one
IS NEVER DECISIVE — the stop condition is read literally. [S2]

WHAT THIS SESSION CONFIRMED FROM SOURCE. The compiled table has EXACTLY ONE
ROW: one operator, ONE published identifier, and SIXTEEN issuing names. The
version string is a date-stamped identifier. The only other mentions of an
operator in the file are the lookup's return type and the lookup itself. [R1]

THE SOURCING IS PINNED THREE WAYS AND DISCLOSED AS INCOMPLETE. Three statements
were used: the authority's own policy document saying what its record identifier
is; its own public documentation page saying the same; and its chain-of-trust
page listing each intermediate with its status and validity. Each was retrieved
on 2026-10-13, fetched BOTH BY BRANCH AND BY COMMIT AND COMPARED BYTE FOR BYTE
before being hashed, and each is recorded with its upstream commit and its own
digest. [S2]

AND THE CAVEATS ARE STATED RATHER THAN BURIED. [S2]

  1. THE AUTHORITY'S CANONICAL SITE WAS UNREACHABLE from the build environment,
     the egress proxy refusing the connection. Everything was therefore read
     from pinned mirrors of the authority's own repositories.
  2. THE POLICY REPOSITORY'S OWN NOTICE SAYS IT IS WHERE THE DOCUMENT IS WORKED
     ON, and that no revision is official until posted on the site that could
     not be reached. A full clone confirmed the commit used IS the repository
     head, that its title matches the current version, that there are no later
     commits, and that the identifier line has been UNCHANGED SINCE A 2023
     REVISION. But THE POSTED DOCUMENT'S TEXT WAS NEVER READ, and the record
     says the claim rests on a version number matching rather than on a
     comparison of texts. A reviewer is asked to confirm both against the live
     pages.
  3. THE EXPIRED INTERMEDIATES AND THE ROOTS ARE DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED, because
     they cannot issue and a certificate chaining through an expired
     intermediate would not be served with a verified chain. A TEST PINS THEIR
     ABSENCE, so they cannot creep back in.

THREE OTHER AUTHORITIES WERE DELIBERATELY NOT COMPILED, AND THE REASON IS THE
BEST ARGUMENT IN THE SLICE FOR A SHORT TABLE. For each, the authority's own
document was unreachable and the available secondary sources DISAGREED WITH ONE
ANOTHER about which identifiers it accepts. One would have had no sourced
issuing-name list at all. They are written up as a reviewer-ready candidate
table MARKED UNVERIFIED AND KEPT OUT OF THE CODE, and the record notes that
each would in any case be a multi-identifier authority, which this check never
judges decisively. [S2]

A TABLE THAT ABSTAINS IS SAFE; A TABLE THAT GUESSES IS NOT. That is the whole
design, and two mutants pin it: one makes the lookup return a guess for an
unknown name, the other adds an unverified name to the table. BOTH ARE CAUGHT.
[S2]

AND THE TABLE'S MAINTENANCE IS AN OPEN QUESTION THE RECORD RAISES ITSELF. New
intermediates will be unlisted and therefore abstain, which is safe but reduces
coverage; the listed ones expire in 2027. A REVIEW CADENCE AND AN OWNER FOR THE
TABLE ARE NEEDED AND DO NOT EXIST. [S2]

5. THE FIRST P1 — A WILDCARD JUDGED AGAINST THE WRONG RECORDS
================================================================================

THE DEFECT, STATED PRECISELY. The relevant record set for a wildcard name is
determined from THE PARENT NAME, and at that parent a separate record type
governing wildcard issuance TAKES PRECEDENCE over the general one. The free
record walk starts at the exact authorised host. So for a site whose certificate
covers it ONLY through a wildcard one label up, the walk examined THE SITE'S OWN
record set, while renewal of that certificate is governed by THE PARENT'S. [S2]

WHY IT WAS A P1 RATHER THAN AN ABSTENTION BUG. The old code treated coverage
through a wildcard name as sufficient and compared on the general record type
whenever no wildcard-specific record appeared in the set it had walked. SO IT
PRODUCED A CONFIDENT PASS OR A CONFIDENT WARNING FROM RECORDS THAT DO NOT GOVERN
THE CERTIFICATE. A wrong confident answer, not a missing one. [S2]

THE REPRODUCERS WERE WRITTEN FIRST AND THEY FAILED. Thirteen cases using a
certificate whose only name is a wildcard. Before the fix: SEVEN FAILED, SIX
PASSED — and the six that passed are the controls and the cases where the facts
were unreadable anyway. After the fix: THIRTEEN PASSED. [S2]

WHAT EACH CASE DID BEFORE AND AFTER. A wildcard-only certificate against the
host's own record set returned a PASS when the records permitted the authority
and a WARNING when they named a different one — BOTH FALSE, both now
undetermined with a coverage-only reason. The same with the record set inherited
from the parent, in three shapes: PASS before, undetermined now. An exact name
accompanied by ANY wildcard name, in four orderings against two record sets:
PASS before, undetermined now with a wildcard-present reason. A wildcard subject
name beside plain names: the same. An exact name whose list sits AT the
transport's capture bound: PASS before, undetermined now with an
incomplete-list reason. A non-string subject: PASS before, undetermined now
because the facts are unreadable. [S2]

AND THE CONTROLS STAYED DECISIVE, WHICH IS WHAT MAKES THE FIX A GATE AND NOT A
BLANKET. An exact name only, against its own or an inherited record set; several
exact names with no subject name; and an exact name with a wildcard-specific
record present — all UNCHANGED. [S2]

THE RULE NOW, IN ONE SENTENCE. A comparison is made only when the certificate
names the host EXACTLY, carries NO wildcard name at all, and its name list is
BELOW the transport's capture bound; otherwise the answer is undetermined with
one of three new closed reasons. This SUBSUMES the earlier, narrower
wildcard-record gate, whose reason token was REMOVED. NO LOOKUP OR NETWORK
OPERATION WAS ADDED, and the hosts, record owner and counts established before
the gate are still recorded. [S2]

THE BINDING ORACLE ROW FOR THE WILDCARD SITUATION KEEPS ITS STATUS AND CHANGES
ITS REASON, which is exactly the right outcome: the expectation was always an
abstention, and now it abstains for a better-founded reason. [S2]

AND THE TESTS THAT HAD ENCODED THE DEFECT WERE CORRECTED, NOT DELETED. Two
existing tests had asserted the old confident answers. Both now assert the
abstention, and the independent restatement used by the seeded sweep was updated
to abstain on any wildcard certificate. FIVE MUTANTS PIN THE NEW GATE: removing
the wildcard-only gate, removing the any-wildcard gate, counting wildcard
coverage as exact, ignoring a wildcard subject, and removing the list-bound
gate. ALL FIVE ARE CAUGHT. [S2]

6. THE SECOND P1 — A NAME IS NOT AN IDENTITY
================================================================================

THE FINDING. The check mapped the issuing certificate's common name to an
authority. THAT NAME IS CHOSEN BY THE ISSUER AND VERIFIED BY NO ONE. The
counterexample in the record is concrete: a different publicly trusted authority
operates an intermediate with the same short common name and issues the site's
certificate. The chain verifies against a trusted root, so the authorisation
flag is true; the transport records that name; the table reads it as the first
authority. THE CHECK THEN ANSWERS CONFIDENTLY ABOUT THE WRONG AUTHORITY. [S2]

THE WITHDRAWN MITIGATION IS THE PART WORTH DWELLING ON. The first version of the
record had listed the requirement of a verified chain as a mitigation for
exactly this risk. The review showed the reasoning was wrong: A VERIFIED CHAIN
PROVES THAT SOME TRUSTED ROOT VOUCHES FOR THE ISSUING KEY, AND SAYS NOTHING
ABOUT WHICH OPERATOR HOLDS IT. The sentence is marked withdrawn where it stands,
rather than edited away. [S2]

EVERY AVAILABLE FACT WAS EXAMINED AND NONE HELPS. The record walks the
transport's captured certificate fields one at a time: the issuer name is the
ambiguity itself; the authorisation flag accepts any trusted root; the
certificate's own subject and names describe the site, not the issuer; the key
type, size and curve are AT MOST A CONSISTENCY HINT that another authority's
intermediate of the same name could satisfy; the validity dates are a weak hint
and not an identity; the protocol, cipher and peer are irrelevant.
CONCLUSION: NO EXISTING FIELD CAN DISAMBIGUATE THE ISSUER WITHOUT CHANGING THE
FREE TRANSPORT LAYER. [S2]

AND CAPTURING MORE OF THE NAME WOULD NOT HAVE HELPED EITHER, WHICH IS THE
INSIGHT. The transport deliberately reads only a shallow view of the peer
certificate and never reads the issuer's full distinguished name or public key.
Capturing the issuer's organisation name as well WOULD STILL BE A
SELF-ASSERTED NAME. The problem is not that the name is short; it is that a name
is not evidence. [S2]

THE RECORD THEN SKETCHED WHAT REAL EVIDENCE WOULD LOOK LIKE, AND DID NOT BUILD
IT. In the SAME handshake, with no new connection, compute a digest of the
public key of the first certificate in the presented chain for which the site's
certificate is BOTH issued by it AND verifies against its key — which
CRYPTOGRAPHICALLY BINDS the site's certificate to that key. Carry it as a
paid-only fact beside, never inside, the fields the free scan consumes. Key the
table by those digests, computed from the authority's own published
intermediates. Require the name to agree as well, and NEVER let a name alone
decide. [S2]

THE DESIGN WAS EVEN PARTLY MEASURED, WHICH IS WHY IT IS CREDIBLE. The
authority's own page states that an authority is most correctly thought of as A
KEY AND A NAME, and that one authority may be represented by several
certificates sharing one subject and public key. The record reports fetching
all the published certificate files and finding that the sixteen listed
intermediates have SIXTEEN DISTINCT KEY DIGESTS, and that each cross-signed
pair SHARES ONE KEY DIGEST WHILE HAVING DIFFERENT CERTIFICATE FINGERPRINTS —
which is precisely why the key, not the certificate, is the stable identifier.
NONE OF THIS IS COMPILED INTO THE CODE. [S2]

BUT IT CHANGES THE FREE TRANSPORT, SO IT IS NOT THIS SLICE'S TO DO. It would
need the owner's approval and its own byte-invariance proof, and the record says
so. [S2]

THE THREE OPTIONS PUT TO THE OWNER. [S2]

  A  ACCEPT the residual risk and keep deciding, with confidence lowered and
     copy saying the authority was identified by its issuing name. Cheapest,
     and A DELIBERATE ACCEPTANCE OF A KNOWN FALSE-ATTRIBUTION PATH.
  B  NEVER DECIDE on a name alone. Every name-only identity abstains. The two
     binding oracle rows would no longer hold as runtime expectations, SO IT
     NEEDS AN OWNER-APPROVED AMENDMENT OF THE RECORD. The check would then
     never be decisive until option C exists.
  C  BUILD THE IDENTITY EVIDENCE. Decisive only on a key-verified issuer. Needs
     a free transport change, approved and proven byte-invariant.

THE IMPLEMENTING SESSION RECOMMENDED C AS THE CONDITION FOR THE CHECK EVER
BECOMING USER-VISIBLE, and left the behaviour unchanged pending the decision.
NO OPTION WAS IMPLEMENTED BEFORE THE OWNER SPOKE. [S2]
